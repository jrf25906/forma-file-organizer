# Forma Brand Voice Guide

Last updated: 2026-03-31

## Brand Position

Forma is a file organizer for people who are overwhelmed by digital clutter and have been burned by automation they couldn't see or reverse. The product's differentiator is **control without anxiety** — every feature exists to make file organization visible, reversible, and trustworthy.

Forma is not competing on beauty, power, or AI magic. It competes on **trust**: the user sees everything before it happens, can undo recent batches when something was wrong, and never has to wonder what the app did behind their back.

## Core Messaging

**Primary tagline:** A file organizer for people who gave up on file organizers.

**Eyebrow/qualifier:** For the perpetually messy.

**One-sentence pitch:** Tell Forma where your files should go — in plain English, not regex. Preview the batch. Undo the recent batch if something was wrong.

**About tagline (in-app):** File organization you can actually trust.

## The Three-Step Model

Use this framework consistently across all surfaces — website, onboarding, marketing, support:

1. **Write a rule** — Describe what you want in plain English. No regex, no scripts.
2. **Preview the batch** — See every file that matched before anything moves. Uncheck what shouldn't.
3. **Undo the batch** — If something was wrong, reverse the recent batch in Forma without moving everything back by hand.

Do not use the older Watch → Match → Organize framework. It describes the system's internals, not the user's experience.

## Voice Principles

### Be specific, not generic

Bad: "Forma helps you organize your files efficiently."
Good: "Your desktop has 400 screenshots on it right now. Don't look. Just write a rule."

Bad: "Create your first rule to automatically organize files."
Good: "'PDFs with invoice in the name go to Finances.' That's a real rule. No twelve-tab settings panel."

Generic productivity language ("streamline your workflow," "boost productivity," "take control") signals that we don't know who our user is. We do.

### Lead with empathy, not capability

Start from the user's reality — they're overwhelmed, they've tried other tools, they're skeptical. Acknowledge the mess before describing the solution.

Bad: "Forma offers powerful rule-based file organization."
Good: "That folder you've been meaning to clean since 2023? One rule handles it."

### Name what we don't do

Our audience has been burned by opaque automation. Explicitly calling out what Forma avoids builds trust faster than listing features.

Examples that work:
- "No account. No cloud. No 'Pro tier' that unlocks the features you actually need."
- "No daemon, no menu bar icon, no silent surprises."
- "No twelve-tab settings panel."
- "No 'we take privacy seriously' blog post."

### Be wry, not clever

Humor should come from recognition ("Screenshot 2024-01-15 at 3.42.17 PM.png") not from wordplay or puns. The user should feel seen, not entertained.

The footer blurb is a good benchmark: "Built by someone who got tired of seeing Screenshot 2024-01-15 at 3.42.17 PM.png fifty times on their desktop."

### Trust signals come first

In onboarding, permission flows, and any context where the user might hesitate, lead with safety before capability:
1. Files never leave your Mac. Everything is local.
2. Review first, then undo the recent batch if it was wrong.
3. No data sent anywhere. No cloud. No tracking.

## Audience

**Primary:** People with chronic file clutter who have given up on maintaining organization systems. This includes (and the SEO explicitly targets) people with ADHD and executive function challenges.

**What they share:** They're not lazy — they've tried and failed at rigid folder hierarchies. They're skeptical of automation because past tools either did too much silently or required too much configuration upfront.

**What they need to hear:** That this tool starts from where they are (messy), not where they should be (organized). That they can see what it will do and undo recent batches when something was wrong. That it doesn't require a Ph.D. in folder taxonomy to use.

## Tone Across Surfaces

| Surface | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Website homepage | Empathetic, specific, slightly wry | "Your files are already out of control." |
| In-app onboarding | Same warmth as homepage, trust-forward | "For the perpetually messy" |
| Settings/UI labels | Clear and direct, no filler | "Show Notifications" (not "Manage your notification preferences") |
| Error messages | Honest and actionable | "Permission denied for ~/Documents. Grant access in System Settings > Privacy & Security." |
| Blog/guides | Practical and opinionated, same voice as homepage | Not generic SEO content — write like the homepage talks |
| Support page | Direct, no ticket-maze energy | "Send what happened, what you expected, and your macOS version." |
| FAQ | Anticipate real anxiety, answer directly | "Will it delete my files?" → "No. Forma only moves files — never deletes." |

## Feature Naming

Use these terms consistently:

| Concept | Preferred term | Avoid |
|---------|---------------|-------|
| Organization logic | **Rules** | Smart Rules, Organization Rules |
| Rule language | **Plain English** or **plain-language rules** | Natural language rules, NLP rules |
| Pre-move review | **Preview** | Review, inspection |
| Reversal | **Undo** | Rollback, revert |
| File grouping | **Batch** | Group, set, collection |
| ML suggestions | **Suggestions** (only when user-facing) | Smart features, AI features, predictions |

## Words and Phrases to Avoid

- "Streamline," "optimize," "boost," "supercharge" — productivity-app filler
- "Powerful," "robust," "comprehensive" — means nothing
- "Smart" as a prefix (Smart Rules, Smart Features) — overloaded and vague
- "Tidy" as a verb — cleaning-product energy
- "Give your files form" — legacy tagline, retired
- "Leverage," "utilize" — just say "use"
- "It's important to note" — just say the thing

## The Name

"Forma" connotes shape, beauty, structure — which originally reflected a design-first positioning. The product has evolved past that. The website copy counterbalances the name's aesthetic connotation by being blunt, specific, and empathetic rather than elegant. Follow that lead. Don't write copy that leans into the "form/beauty" etymology. The name is fine; the voice does the positioning work.
